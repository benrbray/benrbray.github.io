# Modeling Fluids

Assuming the field has constant pressure and temperature, it suffices to model only the following physical quantities:

* The velocity $\mathbb{u} \in \R^2$ at each point
* The **pressure** $p \in \R$ at each point


# Navier-Stokes Equation

The first equation states that the velocity field is **divergence-free**.  Physically, this means that the fluid is **incompressible**.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\nabla \cdot \mathbb{u} = 0
&& \text{(divergence-free)}
\end{aligned}
$$

The second equation describes how velocity changes with time, according to pressure $p$, uniform density $\rho$, kinematic viscosity $\nu$, and external forces $\mathbb{f}$.

$$
\frac{\partial \mathbb{u}}{\partial t}
= -(\mathbb{u} \cdot \nabla) \mathbb{u}
- \frac{1}{\rho} \nabla p
+ \nu \nabla^2 \mathbb{u}
+ \mathbb{f}
$$
